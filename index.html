The paper

Abstract

Zero-shot singing voice conversion (SVC) aims to transform the lead vocal of a song to match the timbre of a reference recording while preserving the source lyrics, melody, and accompaniment. Representative open-source SVC systems primarily focus on vocal conversion, with optimization and evaluation centered on the converted vocal rather than the resulting mix. Their output loudness can also follow the reference rather than the source, making it difficult to maintain the vocal–accompaniment loudness balance during mixing. To address these limitations, we propose InSong-SVC, which conditions vocal generation on the retained accompaniment and optimizes the system for the final mix. Loudness-Controlled SFT combines explicit loudness conditioning with random reference-gain augmentation to encourage output loudness to follow the source vocal. Mix-aware GRPO adds rewards computed on the converted mix to optimize the quality of the final mix. The resulting system supports mixing by direct addition and achieves lower mix transcription error and higher SongEval and SongBench scores than the evaluated baselines on Chinese and English benchmarks.
